## 💼 LinkedIn

Most blockchain governance has a free rider problem: voting costs you nothing, so nobody thinks twice before clicking "Adopt."

Caldera is an experiment to change that.

It's a governance dapp on the Internet Computer where you put ICP behind your conviction on NNS proposals. Commit tokens to ADOPT or REJECT — if the community clears the participation threshold, a Leader Neuron votes the winning side and all committed ICP is burned to cycles that literally keep the app running. If the threshold isn't met, everyone gets refunded.

The result: votes carry real weight, and the more the app is used, the longer it sustains itself.

It's fully on-chain, sign-in with Internet Identity, and supports multi-token commits — ckBTC, ckETH, ckUSDC, ckUSDT all work via an internal swap desk. There's also pooled staking, a lossless lottery, community idea board, and more.

Worth exploring if you care about mechanism design, on-chain governance, or building on ICP.

## 💬 Discord Forum Post

**Title:** Caldera — burn ICP to vote on NNS proposals (skin-in-the-game governance)

Hey folks, wanted to share something I've been building: **Caldera**, a governance dapp on ICP.

The core idea: when you vote on NNS proposals, you commit ICP to signal conviction (ADOPT or REJECT). If enough people participate and the threshold is hit, a Leader Neuron casts the vote and all committed ICP is burned to cycles — which literally fund the canister keeping the app alive. Miss the threshold? Everyone gets refunded.

It's a shot at fixing the "free vote" problem in most on-chain governance — your vote actually costs something, which hopefully means it means something.

**What's in there:**
- Burn voting on active NNS proposals (weighted by ICP committed)
- Multi-token commits — ckBTC, ckETH, ckUSDC, ckUSDT all supported
- Pooled staking (participate without giving up custody)
- Lossless lottery, on-chain payout history
- Community idea board so you can signal what to build next
- Progressive onboarding — browse anonymously, then unlock more as you go
